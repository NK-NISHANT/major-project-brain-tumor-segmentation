2. Core Objective, Clinical Problem & Final Deliverables

2.1 Formal Academic Title

"Reliability-Aware AI Agent for Brain Tumor Segmentation Using Multimodal MRI, 2D CNNs, and Selective Uncertainty Calibration"

2.2 The Real Clinical Problem

Deep learning models for medical image segmentation typically output raw softmax probabilities that are severely miscalibrated—models predict segmentations with 95%+ confidence on ambiguous tumor boundaries where the actual spatial overlap (Dice score) is catastrophic ($Dice < 0.50$). In neuro-oncology surgical planning, an undetected lesion boundary or a hallucinated tumor volume has immediate patient safety consequences.

Furthermore, a single model cannot be universally trusted across all anatomical slice locations. In 2D axial MRI, central slices with large, distinct tumor cores behave very differently from peripheral slices near the skull vertex or base, where contrast is low and volume is minimal.

2.3 The Three Core Contributions of Our Solution

  1. Lightweight Champion Segmentor (ResUNet-2D): We designed a custom 2D Residual U-Net utilizing pre-activation residual blocks, Group Normalization ($G=8$), and bias-free convolutions. With only 8.11M parameters and 538 MB peak VRAM, it outperforms heavy transfer learning architectures while remaining fully trainable on consumer laptop hardware.
  2. Post-Hoc Probability Calibration: We implement parameter-efficient Temperature Scaling ($T^* = 2.8808$), optimized via L-BFGS on hold-out validation cases. This reduces Expected Calibration Error (ECE) by 60.1% ($8.61\% ightarrow 3.44\%$) while strictly preserving spatial argmax predictions ($Dice = 0.8641$ invariant).
  3. Selective Prediction & Radiological Triage Agent: Operating on calibrated predictive entropy, our decision agent filters out the 15.94% lowest-quality, most degraded slices (mean Dice 0.4994) and defers them for human radiologist review, while automatically approving 84.06% of high-confidence slices (mean Dice 0.8303).

4.2 Why Models 6, 7 & 8 Were Formally Scoped Out

  • TransUNet (CNN-Transformer Hybrid): Vision Transformer self-attention blocks on $(240 imes 240)$ feature maps require $\ge 12$ GB VRAM for batch size 2. On an 8 GB laptop GPU, attempting TransUNet triggers CUDA Out-Of-Memory errors or forces severe patch downsampling that destroys fine tumor boundaries.
  • 3D U-Net (Volumetric Convolutions): True 3D convolutions on $(155 imes 240 imes 240)$ volumes require 16+ GB VRAM. Sub-volume patch cropping ($96 imes 96 imes 96$) introduces severe boundary stitching artifacts and takes over 40 GPU-hours per run.
  • MedSAM (Medical Segment Anything Model): Evaluated following a supervisor inquiry. MedSAM has 360M+ parameters, requires manual point/box prompts per slice (cannot autonomously segment 155 slices per patient), and produces uncalibrated confidence outputs.

7. The Reliability Agent: Probability Calibration & Selective Triage

The core contribution of our Major Project is the transformation of a raw segmentation CNN into a **Reliability-Aware Clinical Decision Pipeline**.

7.1 Post-Hoc Temperature Calibration (L-BFGS Optimization)

Standard deep networks produce overconfident logits $z(x)$. We apply post-hoc Temperature Scaling by finding a single scalar $T > 0$ such that calibrated probabilities are given by: $$\hat{p}_i = rac{\exp(z_i / T^*)}{\sum_j \exp(z_j / T^*)}$$ Optimized via L-BFGS on 5,020,730 voxels across the 50-case calibration cohort (`Val_cal`), we obtained: $$T^*_{ ext{ResUNet}} = \mathbf{2.8808}, \quad T^*_{ ext{UNetResNet34}} = \mathbf{2.6267}$$

Out-of-Sample Probability Calibration Performance (105 Unseen Evaluation Cases, 311,546 Voxels):

Configuration Temperature ($T$) ECE (Expected Error) MCE (Max Error) NLL (Log Loss) Mean Dice
ResUNet Uncalibrated 1.0000 8.61% 32.54% 1.0324 0.8641
ResUNet Calibrated 2.8808 3.44% (-60.1%) 7.30% (-77.6%) 0.4430 (-57.1%) 0.8641 (Identical)
UNetResNet34 Uncalibrated 1.0000 10.98% 37.82% 0.7584 0.8535
UNetResNet34 Calibrated 2.6267 4.96% (-54.8%) 17.28% (-54.3%) 0.3659 (-51.8%) 0.8535 (Identical)

Crucial Property: Temperature scaling is an order-preserving monotonic transformation. Therefore, the argmax prediction is strictly preserved, and Dice scores are mathematically identical ($0.8641$ before and after calibration).

7.2 Selective Prediction & Clinical Triage Policy

Rather than forcing the model to make hazardous predictions on ambiguous slices, the agent computes the **calibrated foreground predictive entropy**: $$H(x) = - rac{1}{\log(4)} \sum_{c=1}^{4} \hat{p}_c(x) \log \hat{p}_c(x)$$ At operating threshold $ au_{ ext{abstain}} = 0.35$, evaluated across 16,275 slices (6,549 foreground slices) on the 105 held-out evaluation cases:

Triage Results on 105 Unseen Patient Cases (Seed 42) Verified Evidence
  • Automated Acceptance (84.06% Coverage): 5,505 slices are accepted automatically with a high mean Dice of 0.8303.
  • Deferred to Radiologists (15.94% Flagged): 1,044 slices are flagged as unreliable. These flagged slices have an average Dice of only 0.4994.
  • Clinical Value: The agent successfully isolates the most dangerous, degraded segmentations without human intervention, while passing through 84% of scans with high fidelity.
  • Execution Speed: Average inference latency is 18.23 ms per slice (real-time PACS integration).
